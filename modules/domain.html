A. The Housing Loan Lifecycle (Origination to Closure)

NBFC Basics

An NBFC (Non-Banking Financial Company) is a company registered under the Companies Act that provides financial services (loans, deposits, insurance) but does not hold a banking license. An NBFC – Housing Finance Company (HFC) specifically provides home loans and is regulated by the National Housing Bank (NHB) under the oversight of Reserve Bank of India (RBI).

  1. Lead Generation: Customer inquiry from DSA (Direct Selling Agent), branch walk-in, digital channel, or referral.
  2. Application & KYC: Customer submits application form with documents — PAN, Aadhaar, income proof, bank statements, property documents. KYC verification done with CKYC registry.
  3. Credit Appraisal: Underwriting team assesses creditworthiness — CIBIL/bureau score check (minimum 650-700), income verification, FOIR (Fixed Obligations to Income Ratio, should be < 50-60%), existing loan exposure check.
  4. Sanction: Loan approved with terms — sanctioned amount, interest rate (fixed/floating), tenure (up to 30 years), processing fee. Sanction letter issued.
  5. Legal & Technical: Legal team verifies property ownership chain and title. Technical team conducts property valuation for LTV calculation.
  6. Disbursement: Funds released — either full disbursement (ready property) or tranche-based (under construction). Mortgage/charge created on property.
  7. Repayment & Servicing: EMI collection starts (usually via NACH/ECS auto-debit). Interest rate resets (for floating), pre-payment processing, insurance premium tracking.
  8. Closure / Foreclosure: Loan closed after all EMIs paid (regular closure) or early pay-off (foreclosure/pre-payment). Property documents returned. NOC issued.
💡 Interview Tip: When asked "describe the loan lifecycle," walk through all 8 stages and mention which systems handle each stage: LOS (stages 1-5), LMS (stages 6-8), and CRM (customer interactions across all stages).

2 What is the difference between a Login, Sanction, and Disbursement in the lending context? Why do these stages have separate tracking? ▼

Login: The formal acceptance of a loan application — all documents collected, application entered into LOS. This is the first data point for TAT (Turn Around Time) tracking.

Sanction: Credit committee approves the loan with specific terms. Not all logins convert to sanctions (rejection rate could be 30-40%). The Login-to-Sanction conversion rate is a key business KPI.

Disbursement: Actual funds released to the customer/seller. Not all sanctions convert to disbursements — a customer might choose a competitor or property deal might fall through. Sanction-to-Disbursement conversion rate is another critical KPI.

Why separate tracking:

  • Funnel analytics: Business needs to see the conversion funnel — 1000 leads → 500 logins → 350 sanctions → 300 disbursements.
  • Revenue recognition: Processing fees are earned at sanction, but interest income starts only at disbursement.
  • Regulatory reporting: NHB requires separate reporting of sanctions vs. disbursements, including pipeline amounts.
3 What is a construction-linked disbursement? How would you design the data model to track tranche-based disbursements? ▼

Construction-Linked Disbursement: For under-construction properties, the loan amount is released in installments (tranches) aligned with construction milestones — typically 4-6 disbursements:

  • Tranche 1 (20%): On booking — foundation completion
  • Tranche 2 (20%): Plinth level
  • Tranche 3 (25%): Superstructure completion
  • Tranche 4 (20%): Finishing work
  • Tranche 5 (15%): Possession/OC received

Data Model:

CREATE TABLE fact_disbursement_tranche (
    tranche_id          BIGINT IDENTITY,

    tranche_number      INT,         -- 1,2,3,4,5
    tranche_amount      DECIMAL(15,2),
    cumulative_disbursed DECIMAL(15,2),
    remaining_sanctioned DECIMAL(15,2),
    construction_stage  VARCHAR(50), -- 'Foundation','Plinth','Superstructure'
    disbursement_date   DATE,
    processing_status   VARCHAR(20),
    CONSTRAINT pk_tranche PRIMARY KEY (tranche_id)
);

Key queries this enables: "What % of sanctioned amounts are pending disbursement?", "Average time between tranches?", "Loans stuck at partial disbursement?"

B. Key Financial Metrics (EMI, LTV, FOIR)

EMI (Equated Monthly Installment)

EMI is the fixed monthly payment a borrower makes. It consists of two components:

  • Principal Component: The portion that reduces the outstanding loan balance. Increases over time.
  • Interest Component: Calculated on the outstanding balance. Decreases over time.
Formula — EMI Calculation
EMI = P × r × (1+r)^n / ((1+r)^n - 1)

Where:
  P = Principal loan amount
  r = Monthly interest rate (annual rate / 12 / 100)
  n = Tenure in months

Example: Loan = ₹50,00,000 | Rate = 8.5% p.a. | Tenure = 20 years
  P = 5000000
  r = 8.5 / 12 / 100 = 0.007083
  n = 240 months

  EMI = 5000000 × 0.007083 × (1.007083)^240 / ((1.007083)^240 - 1)
  EMI ≈ ₹43,391

Total payment = 43,391 × 240 = ₹1,04,13,840
Total interest = ₹1,04,13,840 - ₹50,00,000 = ₹54,13,840

LTV (Loan to Value Ratio)

LTV = (Loan Amount / Property Value) × 100

  • Determines how much of the property value is being financed.
  • RBI/NHB guidelines: Maximum LTV for home loans up to ₹30 lakhs = 90%; ₹30-75 lakhs = 80%; above ₹75 lakhs = 75%.
  • Lower LTV = Lower risk for the lender (more equity/skin-in-the-game from borrower).
  • LAP typically has lower LTV (50-70%) because it's a general-purpose loan secured by property.

FOIR (Fixed Obligations to Income Ratio)

FOIR = (All existing EMIs + proposed EMI) / Monthly Income × 100

  • Acceptable FOIR: typically 40-60% for salaried, 35-50% for self-employed.
  • If FOIR > threshold, the loan either gets rejected or the sanctioned amount is reduced.

1 Write a SQL query to calculate the monthly EMI schedule for a ₹50 lakh loan at 8.5% for 20 years, showing principal and interest breakdown. ▼
SQL — EMI Amortization Schedule
WITH RECURSIVE amortization AS (
    -- Base case: Month 0 (loan start)
    SELECT
        0 AS month_num,
        5000000.00 AS opening_balance,
        0.00 AS emi,
        0.00 AS interest_component,
        0.00 AS principal_component,
        5000000.00 AS closing_balance
    UNION ALL
    -- Recursive: Each subsequent month
    SELECT
        month_num + 1,
        closing_balance,
        43391.00,  -- Pre-calculated EMI
        ROUND(closing_balance * 0.085 / 12, 2),
        43391.00 - ROUND(closing_balance * 0.085 / 12, 2),
        closing_balance - (43391.00 - ROUND(closing_balance * 0.085 / 12, 2))
    FROM amortization
    WHERE month_num < 240
)
SELECT month_num, opening_balance, emi,
       interest_component, principal_component, closing_balance
FROM amortization
WHERE month_num > 0;

Key insight: In the early months, ~70% of EMI goes to interest. By the last few years, ~90% goes to principal. This is why pre-payment early in the loan tenure saves the most interest.

2 A customer earns ₹1,20,000/month and already pays ₹15,000 EMI for a car loan. The FOIR threshold is 50%. What is the maximum EMI this customer can afford for a home loan? ▼

Calculation:

  • Monthly Income = ₹1,20,000
  • Maximum FOIR = 50%
  • Maximum total EMI = ₹1,20,000 × 50% = ₹60,000
  • Existing car EMI = ₹15,000
  • Maximum home loan EMI = ₹60,000 - ₹15,000 = ₹45,000

At 8.5% for 20 years, an EMI of ₹45,000 translates to approximately ₹51.8 lakhs loan eligibility.

Data engineering relevance: You'd build a derived column in the data warehouse: max_eligible_emi = (monthly_income * foir_threshold) - existing_emi_obligations — this feeds into the credit appraisal dashboard in Tableau.

3 What is the difference between GNPA % and NNPA %? Why does the NBFC's management dashboard track both? ▼

GNPA (Gross NPA) % = Total NPA amount / Total outstanding portfolio × 100

This shows the raw asset quality — what percentage of the portfolio has gone bad (90+ DPD).

NNPA (Net NPA) % = (Gross NPA - Provisions) / (Total Outstanding - Provisions) × 100

This shows the net exposure after provisions — the actual risk remaining after the company has set aside money to absorb expected losses.

Why track both:

  • GNPA: Tells you how well the company is originating loans (credit quality). Rising GNPA = bad underwriting decisions.
  • NNPA: Tells you how well the company is provisioning for losses. Low NNPA with high GNPA = company is being conservative (good). High NNPA = underprovisionaged (risky).
  • Regulatory requirement: RBI mandates disclosure of both in quarterly results. Rating agencies (CRISIL, ICRA) use NNPA for credit ratings.
  • Data engineering: Both metrics need to be calculated at portfolio-level, product-level, branch-level, and vintage-level for drill-down analysis in Tableau.

C. Delinquency Management (DPD, NPA Classification)

DPD (Days Past Due)

DPD = Current Date - EMI Due Date (when EMI is unpaid)

DPD is the fundamental metric for measuring loan delinquency. It starts counting from the day after the EMI due date if payment is not received.

DPD Buckets (Standard Industry Classification)

NPA Classification Rules (RBI Guidelines)

  • Standard to NPA: Automatically classified as NPA when DPD crosses 90 days. No human intervention needed — the system must auto-classify.
  • NPA to Standard (Upgrade): When all overdue amounts (principal + interest + penal interest) are fully paid and the account becomes current.
  • Provisioning: Every NPA requires the NBFC to set aside provisions (reserves) against potential losses. These provisions directly reduce profitability.
SQL — DPD & NPA Classification Logic
SELECT
    loan_id,
    emi_due_date,
    payment_date,
    CASE
        WHEN payment_date IS NOT NULL
            THEN DATEDIFF('day', emi_due_date, payment_date)
        ELSE DATEDIFF('day', emi_due_date, CURRENT_DATE)
    END AS dpd,
    CASE
        WHEN dpd = 0 THEN 'X-Current'
        WHEN dpd BETWEEN 1 AND 30 THEN 'SMA-0'
        WHEN dpd BETWEEN 31 AND 60 THEN 'SMA-1'
        WHEN dpd BETWEEN 61 AND 90 THEN 'SMA-2'
        WHEN dpd > 90 THEN 'NPA'
    END AS asset_classification
FROM fact_repayment;

Collections Process

Collections is the process of recovering overdue amounts. It follows a structured escalation:

  • Soft Collection (1-30 DPD): Automated SMS, email, IVR calls. Data: Contact attempts, response rate.
  • Tele-calling (31-60 DPD): Dedicated collection agents make phone calls. Data: Call outcomes (PTP = Promise to Pay, broken PTP, not reachable).
  • Field Collection (61-90 DPD): Physical visits to customer's home/office. Data: Visit reports, PTP amounts, partial payments.
  • Legal Action (90+ DPD): Legal notices under SARFAESI Act (Securitisation and Reconstruction of Financial Assets and Enforcement of Securities Interest Act). Property auction process.
⚠️ Critical: In many interviews, they'll ask "How would you build a collections dashboard?" — Answer: DPD bucket-wise distribution (pie chart), Resolution rate by bucket, PTP conversion rate, Month-over-month roll-rate analysis (how many loans moved from SMA-1 to SMA-2 vs. SMA-1 to Current).

1 Write a SQL query to create a DPD bucket distribution report showing loan count and outstanding amount per bucket. ▼
SQL — DPD Bucket Distribution
WITH latest_dpd AS (
    SELECT loan_id,
           MAX(dpd) AS current_dpd,
           MAX(outstanding_balance) AS outstanding
    FROM fact_repayment
    WHERE emi_due_date <= CURRENT_DATE
    GROUP BY loan_id
)
SELECT
    CASE
        WHEN current_dpd = 0 THEN 'X (Current)'
        WHEN current_dpd BETWEEN 1 AND 30 THEN 'SMA-0 (1-30)'
        WHEN current_dpd BETWEEN 31 AND 60 THEN 'SMA-1 (31-60)'
        WHEN current_dpd BETWEEN 61 AND 90 THEN 'SMA-2 (61-90)'
        WHEN current_dpd > 90 THEN 'NPA (90+)'
    END AS dpd_bucket,
    COUNT(*) AS loan_count,
    SUM(outstanding) AS total_outstanding,
    ROUND(COUNT(*) * 100.0 / SUM(COUNT(*)) OVER(), 2) AS pct_of_portfolio
FROM latest_dpd
GROUP BY dpd_bucket
ORDER BY
    CASE dpd_bucket
        WHEN 'X (Current)' THEN 1
        WHEN 'SMA-0 (1-30)' THEN 2
        WHEN 'SMA-1 (31-60)' THEN 3
        WHEN 'SMA-2 (61-90)' THEN 4
        WHEN 'NPA (90+)' THEN 5
    END;
2 What is a roll-rate analysis, and why is it critical for NBFC risk management? How would you build it as a data engineer? ▼

Roll-Rate Analysis measures the movement (flow) of loans between DPD buckets from one month to the next. It answers: "Of the loans in SMA-1 last month, how many moved to SMA-2, how many cured back to Current, and how many stayed?"

Example Roll-Rate Matrix (March → April):

From \ ToCurrentSMA-0SMA-1SMA-2NPA
Current95%4%0.8%0.1%0.1%
SMA-060%15%20%3%2%
SMA-125%10%20%35%10%
SMA-210%5%5%20%60%
NPA5%2%1%2%90%

How to build it: Snapshot the DPD bucket of every loan on the last day of each month. Create a monthly_dpd_snapshot table. Then join current month snapshot with previous month to calculate the transition matrix.

3 A loan has the following payment history: EMI due on 5th of each month. Paid Jan-5 on time, Feb-5 not paid, Mar-5 not paid, Apr-5 not paid, May-5 paid ₹3 EMIs. What is the DPD on each date? ▼

DPD Calculation:

  • Jan 5: Paid on time → DPD = 0
  • Feb 5: Not paid → DPD starts. On Feb 28: DPD = 23 days
  • Mar 5: Still not paid (Feb EMI overdue) → DPD = 28 days (from Feb 5 due date). Now 2 EMIs overdue.
  • Mar 31: DPD = 54 days (counted from earliest unpaid EMI: Feb 5)
  • Apr 5: 3 EMIs now overdue (Feb, Mar, Apr) → DPD = 59 days
  • Apr 30: DPD = 84 days → SMA-2 category
  • May 5: Customer pays 3 EMIs → ALL overdue cleared → DPD resets to 0

Key Rule: DPD is always calculated from the oldest unpaid EMI due date, not from the most recent. This is critical — many candidates get this wrong. Also, partial payment that doesn't clear the oldest due EMI does NOT reset DPD.

D. Data Governance, Quality & Regulatory MIS

Data Governance Framework

In an NBFC, data governance is non-negotiable because of regulatory scrutiny by RBI and NHB. A Data Engineer must build guardrails into every pipeline.

  • Data Ownership: Every table in the warehouse must have a documented business owner (e.g., Collections team owns fact_collection, Risk team owns the NPA classification logic).
  • Data Lineage: You must be able to trace any number on a Tableau dashboard back to its source system (LOS, LMS, CRM) through every transformation step. Tools: AWS Glue Data Catalog lineage, or manual lineage documentation.
  • Access Controls: Strict separation — analysts can only read from analytics schema. ETL service accounts write to staging and dwh. Nobody except DBAs touches admin. Enforced via Redshift GRANT/REVOKE and IAM roles.

Data Quality Framework

Financial data demands 100% accuracy. Build automated DQ checks into every ETL pipeline:

DQ Check TypeWhat It ValidatesNBFC Example
CompletenessNo unexpected NULLs in critical fieldsloan_id, customer_id, emi_amount must never be NULL
UniquenessNo duplicate primary keysNo duplicate loan_id in fact_loan_account
Referential IntegrityAll FKs exist in parent dimensionEvery branch_id in fact table exists in dim_branch

FreshnessData arrived on timeToday's staging table has rows with etl_load_date = CURRENT_DATE
ReconciliationSource count = Target countMySQL source rows match Redshift staging count (covered in SQL Scenario 6)

Regulatory MIS & NHB/RBI Reporting

An NBFC-HFC must submit periodic returns to the National Housing Bank (NHB) and RBI:

  • NHB Quarterly Returns: Portfolio composition (product-wise, geography-wise), NPA movement (opening balance → additions → recoveries → write-offs → closing balance), asset classification distribution.
  • RBI Monthly Returns: ALM (Asset-Liability Mismatch) statements, exposure reports, large loan reports.
  • Audit Requirements: Complete audit trail of all data changes. Every ETL run must log: rows loaded/rejected, checksums, timestamps, and the IAM role used. This is stored in an etl_audit schema.

Python Script: Automated DQ Check (Boto3 + Redshift)

Python — Data Quality Check via Boto3
import boto3
import json

def run_dq_check():
    # Connect to Redshift Data API (serverless, no driver needed)
    client = boto3.client('redshift-data', region_name='ap-south-1')

    dq_queries = {
        'null_check': """
            SELECT COUNT(*) AS null_loans
            FROM fact_loan_account
            WHERE loan_id IS NULL OR customer_id IS NULL
        """,
        'duplicate_check': """
            SELECT loan_id, COUNT(*) AS cnt
            FROM fact_loan_account
            GROUP BY loan_id HAVING COUNT(*) > 1
            LIMIT 10
        """,
        'freshness_check': """
            SELECT CASE
                WHEN MAX(etl_load_date) = CURRENT_DATE THEN 'PASS'
                ELSE 'FAIL - STALE DATA'
            END AS freshness_status
            FROM staging.stg_repayments
        """
    }

    for check_name, sql in dq_queries.items():
        response = client.execute_statement(
            ClusterIdentifier='nbfc-redshift-cluster',
            Database='nbfc_dwh',
            DbUser='etl_service',
            Sql=sql
        )
        print(f"Submitted DQ check: {check_name} | ID: {response['Id']}")

if __name__ == '__main__':
    run_dq_check()

🧠 Test Your Knowledge — Data Governance & Quality
1 Your ETL pipeline loaded 50,000 rows yesterday but the source system shows 50,012. How do you investigate and resolve this discrepancy? ▼
  1. Check ETL audit logs: Look at the etl_audit table for yesterday's run — how many rows were extracted, how many passed DQ checks, and how many were rejected?
  2. Check STL_LOAD_ERRORS: If data was rejected during COPY, Redshift logs the reason (data type mismatch, encoding error, etc.).
  3. Check for duplicates in source: The source might have 12 duplicate rows that your ETL correctly deduplicated.
  4. Check filter logic: Your ETL might have a WHERE clause that excludes certain records (e.g., soft-deleted rows, test accounts).
  5. Resolution: Build a reconciliation table that logs source_count, target_count, and delta for every run. Set an alert threshold (e.g., >0.1% difference triggers investigation).
2 How would you handle a scenario where the source system schema changes without notice (e.g., a new column is added or a column is renamed)? ▼

Prevention:

  • Schema validation step: Add a pre-load task in your Airflow DAG that compares the incoming file/table schema against expected schema stored in a config file.
  • Glue Crawlers: Schedule Glue Crawlers to detect schema drift. If the crawler detects new columns, send an SNS alert for human review before proceeding.
  • Defensive SQL: Use explicit column lists in your SELECT and COPY statements instead of SELECT *. If a column is renamed, the pipeline fails explicitly (which is better than loading wrong data silently).

Recovery: Add the new column to staging, run ALTER TABLE on the target, backfill the new column, and update all downstream views/MVs.

4 An NHB quarterly return requires NPA movement data (opening → additions → recoveries → write-offs → closing). How would you build this as a data engineer? ▼

Design an NPA Movement Fact Table:

SQL — NPA Movement Report
SELECT
    product_type,
    SUM(CASE WHEN npa_status_prev = 'NPA' THEN outstanding ELSE 0 END) AS opening_npa,
    SUM(CASE WHEN npa_status_prev != 'NPA' AND npa_status_curr = 'NPA'
        THEN outstanding ELSE 0 END) AS additions,
    SUM(CASE WHEN npa_status_prev = 'NPA' AND npa_status_curr = 'Standard'
        THEN outstanding ELSE 0 END) AS recoveries,
    SUM(CASE WHEN npa_status_prev = 'NPA' AND is_written_off = TRUE
        THEN outstanding ELSE 0 END) AS write_offs
FROM fact_npa_monthly_snapshot
WHERE report_quarter = 'Q4-FY2026'
GROUP BY product_type;

Key design: Maintain a monthly snapshot table that captures each loan's NPA status at month-end. Compare consecutive months to derive movement. This requires SCD-style snapshotting — not just current state.

5 How do you implement a data quality alerting system that stops bad data from reaching production dashboards? ▼

Gate-based architecture:

  1. Staging gate: Raw data lands in staging schema. Airflow runs DQ checks (null, duplicate, freshness, range). If ANY check fails → halt pipeline, send SNS/Slack alert, log to dq_failures table.
  2. Promotion gate: Only after ALL DQ checks pass, the Airflow DAG promotes data from staging → dwh schema. This is the "production" layer that Tableau points to.
  3. Dashboard staleness alert: A separate monitoring DAG checks if the dwh layer was updated today. If not, it means the pipeline failed silently — send an alert to the data team AND the business stakeholders.

Result: Business users never see bad or stale data. If something goes wrong, they see yesterday's data (which is correct) while the data team fixes the pipeline.
